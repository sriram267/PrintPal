## 1. Positioning

- **One line:** PrintPal is the first **design-led business OS for print and signage**, built by a designer for an industry that still runs on PDFs and WhatsApp.
- **The gap:** existing print software (shopVOX, PrintPLANR, InfoFlo Print) is deep but "built by engineers, for engineers": dated, slow to set up and US-centric. In India there is no dedicated modern tool. Shops use WhatsApp, Excel or Word templates, paper and Tally.
- **The angle:**
  - the depth of print software with the feel of Linear, Notion or Stripe;
  - working within an hour, in the shop owner's own words;
  - India-first: GST, WhatsApp-style sending, sq ft and running ft, many sites per customer.
- **Public positioning today (website):** *"Quoting software for sign shops and large-format printers in India."* Hero line: *"Quoting that speaks print & signage."* Free during the pilot. The site claims only what is built.
- **Long-term:** a deep vertical suite for print, entering one vertical at a time. Whether to say this publicly is **Open** (file 05, question 13).
- **Product shape (Open):** either one product with every vertical inside (vertical "tabs" switched on per business type), or a parent brand with focused products (PrintPal Sign, PrintPal Wrap …). The plan is to run the pilot, see where the pull is, then decide.

## 2. Who we sell to

### Pilot customers (Decided)
1. **Sign shops and signage fabricators:** acrylic and LED channel letters, non-lit and metal signs, ACP fascias, name boards, pylons and hoardings, CNC / laser cutting, fabrication plus installation.
2. **Large and wide-format printers:** flex banners, vinyl, window graphics, wall murals, standees, backlit displays, canvas. Sold per sq ft / sq m.

Many Indian shops do both under one roof, with a CNC / laser bay, a wide-format printer and a welding bay.

### Ideal customer (a hypothesis from desk research)
- About 15–45 staff and roughly ₹1.5–20 Cr turnover, owning CNC / laser machines. Owner-led, quoting daily.
- Tools today: Tally (accounts), Excel (estimates), WhatsApp (customers, sites, suppliers), CorelDRAW (artwork).
- Pains named in the research:
  - quotes rebuilt by hand every time;
  - enquiries lost in chats;
  - follow-ups forgotten;
  - totals and GST done on a calculator;
  - old quotes hard to find;
  - unbilled design revisions;
  - material waste;
  - unpaid final balances.
- Geography: India-wide. Research hubs are Mumbai, Bengaluru, Delhi NCR, Chennai, Hyderabad, Ahmedabad and Pune. An earlier plan started discovery in Chennai.

> **Caveat:** no shop-owner interviews yet. Validate the profile, the pains and willingness to pay in the pilot (section 6).

- **Leads:** enquiries, customers with many sites and services, suppliers (behind a switch).
- **Settings:** business details; a catalogue of products, materials and finishes (starter set of 33 / 30 / 23); tax slabs (not saved yet); the supplier switch.

### 5.2 Now: before real shop data goes in (proposed; founder to confirm the order)
| # | Item | Why |

### 5.3 Next: during the pilot (Planned; pilot feedback decides the order)

- Edit the **site-survey fields** (mounting height, wall type, power point) and the customer type / payment terms / credit limit. They are stored but not editable.
- **IGST** for inter-state jobs. Today the quote always splits CGST / SGST.
- **Excel import** of existing customers and rate sheets.
- **Supplier price requests** (section 5.5).
- **Code tidy-ups:**
  - keep one copy of the starter catalogue;

### 5.4 Later: Phase 2, the full platform (Planned, after paying pilot users)
- **Vertical tabs:** Signage, Wraps and Products all active.
- **The full workflow:** Enquiry → Quote → **Job** → **Production** → **Dispatch** → **Invoice** → **Payment** → Closed.
- **Jobs and a production board:** Kanban plus calendar, job cards.
- **Artwork and proofing:** a proof sent for approval, with changes tracked.
- **Invoicing and payments:** invoice numbering, advance / milestone payments, receipts, balances, reminders.
- **Dispatch and delivery tracking.**
- **Inventory and purchasing:** stock levels, material use per job, purchase orders.
- **Staff, roles and permissions**, with team members inside one shop.
- **Reports and analytics.**

### 5.5 How supplier price requests would work (Planned)
From the October research. The idea comes from PrintPLANR. Suppliers are chosen per job, not per line.
1. On a quote, the owner picks one or more suppliers from the supplier list.
2. Each supplier gets a unique, no-login link (like a Typeform), shared on WhatsApp or email.
3. The supplier sees the job details and line items, fills in their rates and submits.
4. The answers land in PrintPal. The owner compares them side by side and picks one.
5. The owner adds a margin on top of the supplier's cost, and the customer quote is made as usual.

Today the Quotation page shows a Customer / Supplier Quotation switch, but supplier mode does nothing.

**India compliance** (check every rule and threshold with a CA before building):
- HSN / SAC codes on quote lines.
- E-way bills for dispatches above the legal value threshold (₹50,000 per consignment as researched).
- E-invoicing for bigger shops above the turnover threshold (₹5 Cr as researched).
- **Work with the shop's accounting software, don't replace it.** This stance comes from the Palora plan and hasn't been re-confirmed. PrintPal is the operations layer; accounts stay in Tally Prime / Busy / Zoho Books. Later, export vouchers (sales invoices, receipts, purchase orders) into them.

**Workshop-OS ideas** (from the Palora plan; useful when PrintPal goes deeper into fabrication):
- **The long-term promise: "No job leaves you with less money than you thought."** Protect profit across the whole job, not just quote accuracy. The research claims most margin is lost *after* the quote (unbilled revisions, site surprises, scrap, unpaid balances). This is unvalidated.
- **Milestone payment gates** with configurable splits (50/40/10, 70/30, 30/50/20). For example, don't dispatch until the 40% is paid.
- **Approval-locked job cards with a QR code.**
  - Shop CNC routers and lasers run from USB on standalone controllers, so software can't lock the machine.
  - Instead, the approved artwork file is locked and a printed job card carries a QR code. Operators scan it before cutting.
  - Customer approval links were dropped for the pilot, so revisit how approval would arrive.
- **Smart estimating packs:**
  - signage: letter perimeter, LED module density, power-supply sizing with about 25% headroom, nesting on 8×4 ft sheets;
  - print and display: roll media sq ft, ink, lamination, grommets;
  - fabrication: MS / SS steel weight, welding running ft.
- **Shop records and screens:**
  - an offcut / sheet stock register;
  - Tally Prime / Busy voucher export;
  - an owner's "Today" screen;
  - profit per job;
  - installer / labour advance ledgers;
  - municipal permit and warranty reminders;
  - machine and install-crew scheduling.
- **Cross-shop network:** a sign shop outsourcing print to a print house, and a fabricator doing the frames. A possible B2B network effect.

## 6. Go-to-market

### Today
- **Founder-led outreach**, not SEO. The website exists to look complete and credible.
- **Access:** open. Any shop signs up with one **Continue with Google** button; there is no waitlist or invite step (since 22 Nov 2026).
- **Pilot users:** none recorded in these files. Ask the founder for the current state (file 05, question 8).
- **Pricing:** none shown. "Free during the pilot." The model (per seat, per job, flat monthly) is **Open**.
- **Outreach list:** `product/req/signage_industry/top_signage_companies_india.md` lists **122 Indian signage companies**, segmented into three tiers:
  - Tier 1: 3D letter and neon fabricators (prime pilot candidates);
  - Tier 2: retail display and POSM makers;
  - Tier 3: safety and wayfinding specialists.

### Customer discovery plan (Planned, not started; from the Palora plan)
- Recruit **5 established sign shops as design partners**. Sit with the owner and the estimator.
- **10-job audit:** run each shop's last 10 finished jobs through PrintPal's quote screen, then compare what PrintPal would have quoted with what the job really cost.
- Learn the shop's real numbers: LED module density, power-supply sizing, overhead %.
- See how quickly quotes sent on WhatsApp get answered, and how quickly payments come in.

**Hypotheses to test (all untested):**
| # | Hypothesis |
|---|---|
| H1 | 80–90% of local prepress operators use CorelDRAW, because of cutting-machine compatibility. |
| H2 | Real net margin ends 8–15 points below the quoted margin, because of unbudgeted overhead and electrical sizing. |
| H3 | Quotes sent on WhatsApp close faster than email attachments (the plan guessed 40% faster). The original version assumed a one-tap customer approval link, which was dropped. |
| H4 | Holding installation until the milestone payment clears cuts 60-day unpaid balances by more than half. |

### Channel idea: material distributors (Idea)
- Substrate distributors sell on credit to hundreds of fabricators. Examples named in the research: Maruti Flex Traders, Pioneer Acrylic, Viva ACP.
- The idea: load distributors' rate cards into PrintPal so shop quotes stay accurate. In return, distributors recommend PrintPal to their fabricator customers.
